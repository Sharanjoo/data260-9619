# HW10 Part 3 - human labeling rubric (written before any label or judge score exists)

One whole number per draft, **helpfulness for the shopper who asked the question**, on the same 1-5 scale the judge uses.
Judge each draft from the question (including any database facts that were added to it) and the draft text only.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent: answers exactly what was asked, every detail agrees with the facts given, tells the shopper what to do, safe, clear. |
| 4 | Good: useful, correct and safe, with a small gap (a missing detail, slightly vague). |
| 3 | Fair: partly useful but incomplete or vague, or has a minor error. |
| 2 | Poor: misses most of what was asked, or has a serious error. |
| 1 | Very poor: wrong, unsafe (e.g. says a recalled product is fine to eat), off-topic, or invents facts (lot codes, dates, numbers, phone numbers, web addresses). |

Rules: an honest "that information is not available, here is what to do instead" is a good answer to a question the facts
cannot answer. An invented number or date is a serious error even if the rest is fine. Safety advice that tells the shopper
not to eat or use a recalled product is expected, but a draft that only repeats it and ignores the question is not 5.
Labels are written by hand by the student, are not produced by any model, and are not changed after judge scores exist.
